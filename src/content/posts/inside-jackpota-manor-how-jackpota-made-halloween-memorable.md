Most seasonal events are a banner and a countdown. This October, Jackpota did something closer to a TV season. It gave Halloween a plot, a cast and a weekly release schedule, then dressed the whole site to match.

Halloween night has come and gone, so it's a good moment to look back at how the season was built and why it was fun to follow.

## A Halloween told in five chapters

Jackpota split October into a five-part **Halloween Series**, one chapter at a time, according to its October partner newsletter as reported by [Bonus.com](https://www.bonus.com/news/whats-new-at-jackpota-this-october/). Each chapter brought its own lineup of Halloween games and events:

| Chapter | Title | Dates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Curse Awakes | Oct. 1 to 4 |
| 2 | The Cursed Fortunes | Oct. 5 to 11 |
| 3 | Haunted House of Spins | Oct. 12 to 18 |
| 4 | The Lurking Evil Haunted Reels | Oct. 19 to 25 |
| 5 | Haunted House of Treats & The Escape | Oct. 26 to Nov. 2 |

Read the titles in order and you get the story in miniature. A curse wakes up, gets into the house, takes over the reels, and in the end somebody gets out. The structure did something simple but effective: every Monday felt like a new episode, not just another week.

## Meet the residents of Jackpota Manor

The chapters had a home: **Jackpota Manor**, a short video series on [Jackpota's YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@Jackpotacom). It opened with a teaser, "Something Dark Is Coming," which showed a glowing green path winding up to a gothic mansion.

## Why it worked

Looking back, three design choices made this Halloween stand out:

- **A story with a schedule.** Weekly chapters gave the season a rhythm. There was always a new title, a new episode or a new character to check in on.
- **A recurring cast.** The butler, the doll, the pumpkin-headed host and the Sorceress turned a theme into characters people could recognize from one video to the next.
- **One consistent world.** The logo, the artwork, the videos and the games all spoke the same visual language of glowing green, deep purple and candlelit gold.

None of it asked players to do anything in particular. Like everything at Jackpota, the season was free-to-play entertainment, and the story was simply there to enjoy whenever someone stopped by.
