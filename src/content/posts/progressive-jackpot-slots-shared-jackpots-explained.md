Scroll through our slots lobby and you'll catch something odd. A gorilla in a crown, a joker, a candy swirl and a fishing captain are four completely different games, and all four show the same Gold Coin number on their tiles.

That number is a shared progressive jackpot. **A progressive jackpot is a jackpot whose amount grows as people play and resets to a starting value once it's awarded.** A shared one sits across many games at once, so every linked game displays the same total.

I've spent years building social casino lobbies, and the jackpot meter is still my favorite thing on the screen. It's the one part of a slot that isn't about your game alone. So I wanted to know how much of our own lobby is actually wired to one. We counted.

## What makes a jackpot "progressive"

Start with the opposite. A fixed jackpot is a set amount, or a set calculation, that looks the same every time you open the game. A progressive jackpot moves. Its amount climbs according to its rules, and when it's awarded it drops back to where it started and begins climbing again.

Picture a meter that started the week at one figure and reads higher on Friday. Nobody changed the game. The jackpot grew because people played.

Progressives also differ in how far they reach, and this is the part I find most interesting from a design point of view:

- **One game:** the jackpot belongs to a single slot.
- **A group of games:** one studio links a set of its own titles to the same jackpot.
- **A whole platform:** the jackpot sits above the games entirely.

The tier names you see on these meters are a separate subject. We covered them in our guide to [Mini, Minor, Major and Grand jackpots](/what-are-jackpot-slots-mini-minor-major-grand-explained/).

## The three jackpot layers in our lobby

All three kinds are in our lobby, stacked on top of each other.

The widest is our own [Gold Coins Jackpot](https://www.jackpota.com/promotions/gc-jackpot). Its published terms describe a progressive jackpot with four tiers (Mini, Minor, Major and Community) that grow with every jackpot contribution. Everyone takes part automatically, the contribution comes out of your Gold Coin balance on each game round, and there is no opt-out. The jackpot can trigger at random at any point in a round, and an animation shows which tier it was. The terms page has the full detail, and it's worth a read.

The middle layer is the one I went looking for: jackpots that a game studio runs across a group of its own slots. These are the matching numbers on those tiles.

The third layer lives inside individual games, where a slot has its own jackpot feature with its own rules.

They are separate systems. A game can sit under more than one of them, and the rules for one don't carry over to another.

## We counted: 79 slots share a studio jackpot

In mid-November 2026 our lobby had 2,330 games from more than 60 studios, and 2,229 of them were slots. Of those slots, 79 are linked to a studio-run shared jackpot. That's about 1 in 28.

I expected more. The meters are so visible that they feel like half the lobby, and they turn out to be 3.5% of it.

Only two studios are behind them:

- **Penguin King:** 62 slots on one shared jackpot
- **Playson:** 17 slots on another

That's two jackpots, each one shared by every game in its group. A round on the gorilla game and a round on the joker game are both happening under the same total.

## Most of them are Hold & Win games

Of the 79, 51 are Hold & Win-style games. That fits how those games look. Hold & Win games usually show jackpot labels around the reels already, so a shared meter has an obvious place to sit. If the format is new to you, our [Hold & Win explainer](/hold-win-slots-explained-how-respins-bonus-symbols-and-jackpots-work/) covers the basics.

The rest are a mix: classic fruit-and-sevens games, a few character-led slots, and titles like [Joker Jackpots: Prize Mania](https://www.jackpota.com/games/slots/joker-jackpots-prize-mania) that put the jackpot right in the name.

## It's a recent wave

This surprised me most. Of the 79 games, 73 joined our lobby in 2025 or 2026, and 50 of them arrived this year alone.

Studios are doing this on purpose. In a February 2026 interview with NEXT.io, Playson's product owner described adding a jackpot layer to the studio's proven titles as a parallel family, instead of building brand-new games around it. You keep the game people already know and add a shared meter on top. From a product point of view that's a tidy idea, because the original stays recognizable.

## "Jackpot" in the title isn't the tell

You'd think the name would give it away. It doesn't.

Leaving aside our own Jackpota-branded games, 14 slots in our lobby have "Jackpot" in their title. Only three of them are linked to a studio shared jackpot. The other 11 aren't part of either group, while 76 games that never mention a jackpot in their name are.

A title is a label. What tells you a game is in a shared group is on the tile itself: the pooled games we looked at carry a small badge in the corner, and they all show the same number.

## Reading a shared jackpot on screen

A few things I'd want any reader to know before they start watching one of these meters.

**The same number on different tiles means one jackpot.** There isn't one per game.

**Each layer has its own rules.** Our Gold Coins Jackpot is explained on its terms page. Anything a studio runs is explained in that game's own rules, so the information panel is the place to look.

**A big number isn't a countdown.** Nothing on a meter tells you when a jackpot will be awarded, and a high total doesn't mean it's about to be.

That last one matters to me. A meter is fun to watch, and it's just as fine to ignore.

## The takeaway

A progressive jackpot grows as people play and resets when it's awarded. A shared one does that across a whole group of games, which is why different tiles can show the same number. In our lobby that group is smaller and newer than it looks: 79 slots out of 2,229, from two studios, and most of them arrived in the last two years.
