Mini. Minor. Major. Grand. Those labels can take up plenty of space above a set of reels, but they only tell part of the story.

**Jackpot slots are slot-style games with a jackpot feature alongside their regular gameplay.** A jackpot may have one level or several tiers, each with its own value and conditions. Understanding those conditions makes the screen easier to follow: you know what the labels mean and which feature they belong to.

The useful place to start is the tier names. Then comes a distinction that is easy to miss: a jackpot built into a game and a jackpot offered across a platform can be separate systems.

## What do Mini, Minor, Major, and Grand mean?

These names commonly describe jackpot tiers in ascending order. In a game using all four, Mini sits at the bottom and Grand at the top.

1.  **Mini:** The smallest of the four tiers
2.  **Minor:** Above Mini and below Major
3.  **Major:** Above Minor and below Grand
4.  **Grand:** The highest tier in this naming structure

The labels describe the hierarchy within that system. They do not tell you how a jackpot is triggered, whether its value grows, or how often it appears. “Mini” alone is not evidence that a tier is easier to trigger.

They are not universal names, either. Jackpota’s Gold Coins Jackpot uses **Mini, Minor, Major, and Community**. Grand remains a useful term to understand when reading individual game rules, but it is not the name of Jackpota’s top platform jackpot tier.

## Fixed and Progressive Jackpots: What Changes?

The word **progressive** describes a jackpot whose amount can grow according to its rules. A fixed jackpot follows a set value or calculation rather than an accumulating pool.

That distinction is separate from the tier names. A label such as Major tells you where a tier sits; it does not, by itself, tell you whether the amount is fixed or progressive.

It also helps to distinguish a **starting value** from a **current amount**. The starting value is the system’s baseline. A progressive amount displayed later may be higher. Reading those two figures as interchangeable can make a perfectly ordinary jackpot display look confusing.

Before assuming that a particular symbol triggers a particular tier, check the game’s information panel. The useful explanation will tell you what the symbol does, which feature it belongs to, and what conditions apply. The artwork can catch your eye; the rules explain the connection.

## What to Look for on a Jackpot Screen

You do not need to memorize every detail before the layout starts making sense. Focus on three questions:

-   **Which system am I looking at?** Identify whether the jackpot belongs to the individual game or a separate platform feature.
-   **What does the number represent?** Look for a fixed value, a starting amount, or a current progressive amount.
-   **What triggers the feature?** Use the relevant game rules or jackpot terms rather than guessing from a symbol or tier name.

Those checks give the labels some context. Mini and Major become more than words above the reels: you can place them within the feature you are actually looking at.
